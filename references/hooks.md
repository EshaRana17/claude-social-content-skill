# Hooks and Headlines

A hook is a promise. The content is the payment. A hook that the content
cannot pay off wins one click and loses the follower.

## Step zero: the payoff sentence

Before writing any hook, write one sentence that states what the reader
actually gets: "After reading this, you will know how to price a first
freelance project using last year's hours." Every hook below is tested
against this sentence.

## The five patterns

### 1. Curiosity gap
Open a specific question; the content closes it.

- Formula: [specific observation] + [unexplained part]
- Works because: an open, concrete question is uncomfortable to leave
  unanswered.
- Weak: "You won't believe what happened next."
- Strong: "We cut our onboarding from 9 steps to 3. The step we kept
  surprised the whole team."
- Rule: the gap must be specific. Vague mystery reads as bait.

### 2. Contrarian
Challenge a belief the audience holds, then argue it.

- Formula: "[Common advice] is [wrong / incomplete] for [specific group]."
- Works because: disagreement creates tension and signals a point of view.
- Weak: "Everything you know about marketing is wrong."
- Strong: "Posting every day hurt our newsletter. Here is the schedule that
  replaced it."
- Rule: only use when the content genuinely argues the opposite, with
  reasons. Do not invent a strawman nobody believes.

### 3. Specific result
Lead with a concrete, true outcome.

- Formula: [number] + [outcome] + [timeframe or constraint] + [how hint]
- Works because: specifics are credible and let readers judge relevance fast.
- Weak: "How I grew my business massively."
- Strong: "From 0 to 41 paying customers in 90 days, with no ad spend."
- Rule: the number must be the user's real, verifiable result. Include the
  constraint that makes it honest (timeframe, starting point, context).
  Never round up. Never imply typical results from an outlier.

### 4. Story open
Start in the middle of a moment.

- Formula: [time/place] + [tension] (no setup)
- Works because: narrative pulls readers forward; tension asks "what next?"
- Weak: "Let me tell you a story about my startup."
- Strong: "Two days before launch, our payment provider froze the account."
- Rule: it must be a true story. Composite or hypothetical stories must be
  labelled ("Imagine...", "A pattern I see often...").

### 5. List promise
Commit to a countable, delivered set.

- Formula: [number] + [useful thing] + [for whom / outcome]
- Works because: sets clear expectations and signals skimmable value.
- Weak: "Some tips for writing better."
- Strong: "5 edits that make any LinkedIn post easier to read on a phone."
- Rule: deliver exactly that many items, each substantial. Odd numbers are
  not magic; use the real count.

## Optional supporting patterns

Use these when the five above do not fit; label them in output.

- **Direct address:** "If you run a one-person agency, read this before you
  raise prices."
- **Before/after:** "Old homepage headline: 'Solutions for growth.' New one:
  'Payroll for 5-50 person restaurants.' Sign-ups doubled." (only with real
  data)
- **Mistake confession:** "I wasted three months building a feature nobody
  asked for. What I check now before building anything."

## Platform adaptation

| Platform | Hook location | Length target |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| X | First post, first line | Under ~120 characters for the opening line |
| LinkedIn | First 1-2 lines before "see more" | Under ~200 characters before the fold |
| Instagram carousel | Cover slide headline | 6-10 words |
| Instagram caption | Before ~125-character truncation | One sentence |
| Short video | First 1-3 seconds, spoken and on screen | 5-12 spoken words |
| Threads / Bluesky | First sentence | One line |

## Strength test (score each hook)

1. **Specific?** Contains a concrete noun, number, name or situation.
2. **Relevant?** The target reader instantly knows it is for them.
3. **Tension?** There is a question, conflict or gap.
4. **Honest?** Passes the payoff check.
5. **Short?** Fits the hook location above.

Four or five yeses: strong. Three: rewrite the weakest dimension. Two or
fewer: discard.

## Clickbait test

Reject or rewrite any hook that:

- promises something the content does not contain;
- uses a result the user has not achieved or cannot document;
- implies typical results from an exceptional case;
- uses fake urgency ("Delete this before it is banned");
- uses shock or fear unrelated to the content;
- impersonates news ("BREAKING") for non-news;
- is engagement bait ("Comment 'YES' and I will DM you") - note that a
  genuine opt-in such as "Reply 'guide' and I will send the PDF" is a
  grey area: allowed only if the user will actually send it and the
  platform's rules allow it; flag it.

## Headline variants for long-form

For blog titles, YouTube titles and newsletter subjects, use the same
patterns but favour clarity over cleverness: the reader should know the
topic from the title alone. Keep YouTube titles roughly under 60-70
characters so they are not truncated, and put the key phrase early for
search.

## Output format for hook requests

| # | Hook | Pattern | Why it works | Payoff check |
|---|------|---------|--------------|--------------|

Then "Recommended" (top two with one-line reasons) and "Flagged" (any
option that drifts toward clickbait, with a fix).
