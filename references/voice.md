# Brand Voice

A voice guide describes how the user already sounds at their best, so that
new writing (theirs or Claude's) stays recognisably theirs.

## Collecting samples

- 3-5 pieces the user wrote and likes (posts, emails, a page of copy).
- Optional: 1-2 "never sound like this" examples.
- Prefer variety: one teaching post, one story, one promotional post.
- Do not build a voice guide by imitating another identifiable creator's
  posts; the guide should describe the user.

Fewer than three samples: produce a provisional guide and list what more
samples would clarify.

## Analysis checklist

Work through each dimension and cite short evidence fragments (a few words)
from the samples.

### Tone sliders (1-5)
| Slider | 1 | 5 |
|--------|---|---|
| Formality | Casual | Formal |
| Energy | Calm | Excited |
| Warmth | Reserved | Warm |
| Humour | Serious | Playful |
| Authority | Peer | Expert |
| Directness | Hedged | Blunt |

### Sentence rhythm
- Average sentence length (count words in a few sentences).
- Variation: steady, or short punches between longer lines?
- Fragments: used? How often?
- Paragraph length and line breaks.
- Questions: rhetorical, genuine, or none.

### Vocabulary
- Signature words and phrases (repeated, distinctive).
- Jargon level and whether terms get explained.
- Words the user never uses (for example "synergy", "game-changer").
- Contractions: yes or no.
- British or American spelling.

### Point of view
- I, we, you, or third person.
- Who the reader is assumed to be.

### Formatting habits
- Lists, numbered steps, bold, emoji (frequency), hashtags (count, style),
  capitalisation, punctuation quirks (em dashes, ellipses, exclamation marks).

### CTAs
- How the user asks for action: soft ("if this helps..."), direct ("Book a
  call"), or question-led.

## Voice guide template

```
# Voice guide: [Name / Brand]   (provisional: yes/no)

Voice in one line: [e.g. "A practical friend who has done the work and
tells you the short version."]

## Traits
1. [Trait] - this, not that
   - This: [short example in the voice]
   - Not that: [short counter-example]
2. ...
(3-5 traits)

## Tone sliders
Formality 2 | Energy 3 | Warmth 4 | Humour 2 | Authority 4 | Directness 4

## Rhythm rules
- [e.g. Mostly 8-15 word sentences, one short punch line per paragraph]
- [e.g. Paragraphs of 1-3 lines]

## Word bank
Use: [...]
Avoid: [...]

## Do
- ...
## Don't
- ...

## Formatting
[lists, emoji policy, hashtag style, spelling variant]

## CTA style
[...]

## Before / after
Before: [generic text]
After: [same facts, in the voice]
```

## Applying the guide

1. Read the guide; note the three traits most relevant to the platform.
2. Rewrite the draft for voice: rhythm first, vocabulary second, formatting
   third.
3. Keep every fact, number and claim identical. Voice never changes what is
   true.
4. List the changes: "Split two long sentences (rhythm rule 1); replaced
   'leverage' with 'use' (avoid list)."
5. Platform beats voice on hard limits; voice beats platform habits on style.
   A formal brand can still write a short X post; it just writes it formally.

## Common failure modes

- Overusing signature phrases until they become a tic. Use each at most once
  per post.
- Turning "casual" into slang the user never uses.
- Turning "expert" into jargon. Expert voice explains simply.
- Copying a sample's structure every time. Vary structure; keep voice.
