# Ethics and Disclosure

Honest marketing is also durable marketing: platforms demote manipulation,
regulators fine it, and audiences remember it. This file explains the rules
in SKILL.md with examples. It is guidance, not legal advice; users in
regulated industries or with significant paid partnerships should confirm
requirements with a qualified advisor.

Sources referenced (checked 2026-11-01):
- US FTC, Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in
  Advertising (16 CFR Part 255), revised 2023.
- US FTC, Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and
  Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465), effective October 21, 2024.
- Platform branded-content and spam / platform-manipulation policies.
Other jurisdictions (for example the UK ASA/CAP code and EU consumer law)
have comparable disclosure rules.

## 1. Fake engagement

Not allowed: buying followers, likes, views or comments; engagement pods
that trade likes on schedule; bots; multiple accounts run by one person to
boost posts; coordinated "spontaneous" replies; buying or selling fake
indicators of social media influence (the FTC review rule addresses this
directly).

Offer instead: reply strategies, collaborations with disclosed partners,
genuine community participation, cross-promotion with clear context.

## 2. Fake reviews and testimonials

Not allowed:
- Writing a testimonial and attributing it to a customer, real or invented.
- AI-generated "customer quotes" or composite customers presented as real.
- Rewriting a real review so it says something the customer did not say.
- Offering incentives conditioned on positive reviews.
- Suppressing or hiding negative reviews while presenting the rest as
  complete.

Allowed: quoting real customers with permission; trimming for length
without changing meaning; asking all customers for honest reviews; showing
a typical-results note when highlighting an exceptional outcome.

If the user asks for sample testimonials (for a mockup), label them
clearly as placeholders: `[PLACEHOLDER TESTIMONIAL - replace with a real,
approved quote]`.

## 3. Posing as an unaffiliated customer

Not allowed: the founder, employees, family, investors or paid contractors
posting praise or recommendations as if they were independent users;
"just found this amazing tool" posts from people connected to the company;
replies under competitor posts recommending the user's product without
saying who they are.

Write instead: "I built this", "Disclosure: I work at X", "My company
makes this", "We are biased, but here is why we built it".

## 4. Disclosing material connections

A material connection is anything that could affect how the audience weighs
the recommendation: payment, free products, affiliate commission, discount
codes that earn the poster money, employment, ownership, family ties,
investment.

Disclosure must be:
- **Clear:** plain words such as "Ad", "Sponsored", "Paid partnership with
  [Brand]", "Affiliate link: I earn a commission", "Gifted by [Brand]",
  "I own this company".
- **Prominent:** at the start of the post, before the "see more" fold or
  caption truncation, and both on screen and spoken in video.
- **Not hidden:** not only in a bio, not lost in a block of hashtags, not
  only on a later slide or in comments.
- **Unambiguous:** vague tags like "#sp", "#collab" or "#thanks" alone are
  not enough.

Use the platform's built-in paid-partnership label when available, and keep
the written disclosure as well.

Examples:
- "Ad: [Brand] paid me to try their planner for a month. Honest notes below."
- "I built this. [Product] turns call notes into follow-up emails."
- "Affiliate link below: I earn a small commission at no extra cost to you."

## 5. Engagement bait

Platforms explicitly demote posts that pressure people to interact. Avoid:
- "Comment YES if you agree."
- "Tag 3 friends who need this."
- "Like for A, share for B."
- "Follow and comment to win" without real contest rules.
- Fake controversy to provoke replies.

Allowed: a genuine question you will read and answer; asking people to save
a post if it is genuinely reference material; a real giveaway with rules
that comply with local law and platform policy.

## 6. Invented statistics and sources

- Never write a number the user has not supplied or cannot source.
- Never attribute a quote to a real person who did not say it.
- Never cite a study, survey or report that has not been provided or
  verified.
- Use `[ADD: source]` or `[CHECK: figure]` placeholders.
- Separate the user's own data ("in our 40 customer calls") from general
  claims ("most founders...") and soften the latter or remove it.

## 7. Fake urgency and scarcity

Only true deadlines, true stock limits and true price changes. No countdown
that resets, no "only 3 spots left" unless there are three spots.

## 8. Sensitive claims

Health, medical, mental health, financial returns, legal outcomes, weight
loss and children's products: no outcome guarantees, no before/after claims
without evidence and context, include appropriate caveats, and recommend a
compliance or professional review before publishing.

## 9. Other people and content

- Credit creators whose ideas, data or visuals are used; link to the
  original.
- Do not reproduce long passages of someone else's work.
- Get consent before featuring identifiable people, especially customers
  and minors.
- Do not imitate another creator closely enough to confuse audiences.

## How to decline

Keep it short and useful: say which part cannot be done, why in one line,
and immediately offer the honest version. Example:

"I can't write reviews in your customers' voices, because they would be
presented as real when they are not. I can draft a short email asking your
last 20 customers for an honest review, and a post that shares the real
ones once they arrive."
